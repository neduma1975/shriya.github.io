Drone Belly Mount for Hyperspectral Camera

TL;DR

Belly mount hanging a 1.5 kg MicroHSI 410 hyperspectral camera from a DJI Matrice-class drone — two print iterations, SolidWorks FEA at 20 N (1.59 MPa max stress, 0.015 mm displacement), bench-fitted on drone.

📄 Drone mount PDF (4 pp)↗ 📄 SJSU ARAS Lab team↗

✓ 2 CAD/FEA iterations; 1.59 MPa max stress at 20 N for 1.5 kg camera

Role: CAD, FEA & 3D-print lead — SJSU ARAS Lab (autonomous remote sensing team)

01 / Problem

Mount a ~1.5 kg (14.7 N) MicroHSI 410 hyperspectral camera straight-down (no gimbal) onto the DJI Matrice's damped belly plate without blocking heat sinks, ports, or markings or shifting CG off-axis.

02 / Design process

  1. CADed an L-bracket cradle and base plate in SolidWorks around exact camera envelope, adding draft, fillets, relief cuts, and heat-set inserts
  2. Iter 1 (PLA/PETG): physical fit check exposed rear screw-boss interference + lug hole misalignment → shortened body ~1/8 in, added camera pocket, reprinted at 20% gyroid
  3. Iter 2: thickened mounting tabs and base plate, switched to ribbing over infill, and ran SolidWorks linear static FEA (ABS, 20 N load) on both plate and cradle

03 / Build

Two-part printed cradle and base plate with heat-set inserts; bolt-pattern, port-clearance, and CG verified physically on the DJI Matrice airframe.

04 / Testing & results

Camera & FEA load
~1.5 kg (14.7 N static) tested at 20 N
FEA results (ABS)
1.59 MPa plate / 1.24 MPa cradle, 0.015 mm disp.
Current status
Bench-fitted on DJI body; >30 N pull & flight tests next

05 / What I learned

Isotropic linear FEA misses infill geometry and layer-to-layer anisotropy — physical >30 N load testing is required before flight sign-off.
