Wireless taVNS Biostimulant Earbud

TL;DR

Wireless earbud pairing BLE audio with biphasic taVNS stimulation for motion sickness — KiCad PCB, Phrozen 16K resin housing, machined aluminum electrodes (0.9 Ω vs 165.0 kΩ graphite-silicone, n=6), under $200 BOM.

📄 FSRI poster (1 p)↗

✓ Working prototype <$200; Al electrodes 0.9 Ω vs 165 kΩ composite

Role: Co-developer (3-student FSRI team; Gao Research Group, Caltech) — KiCad PCB, housing, electrodes

01 / Problem

Commercial and experimental taVNS devices are bulky, wired, and cannot simultaneously stream audio. Motion-sickness relief needed a self-contained wireless earbud targeting the concha and cymba concha.

02 / Design process

  1. DigiKey component selection + datasheet verification; breadboard circuit validation
  2. KiCad schematic + layout: USB-C charger, 3.3 V buck/boost, 32 V boost rail, IDAC, quad-switch (biphasic), BLE audio amp
  3. Iterated Phrozen 16K resin housings to fix thin-wall/connector retention; pivoted from cast graphite-silicone electrodes to machined aluminum after 6-trial multimeter comparison

03 / Build

0–5 mA controllable biphasic constant-current stimulation with fail-safes; BLE audio streaming; SLA resin enclosure with machined aluminum concha/cymba-concha electrodes.

04 / Testing & results

Electrode resistance
0.9 Ω (Al) vs 165.0 kΩ composite (~177,000×, n=6)
Prototype BOM
<$200 total (vs traditional clinical taVNS)
Safety ceiling
0–5 mA biphasic constant-current, protected

05 / What I learned

Electrode contact impedance dominated system feasibility — quantitative n=6 resistance testing forced an early pivot off composite silicone.
